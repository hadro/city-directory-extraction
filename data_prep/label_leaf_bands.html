What you are labelling

The vertical extent of the residential listing on this page — where the real name-and-address entries start, and where they stop.

Why it matters: the extraction model never refuses. Feed it a line of advertising and it returns a confidently structured fake person. Knowing where the listing really begins and ends is how we stop that, and your labels are what teach it.

The job, in four steps

  1. Find the dense two-column block of entries. That is the body.
  2. Drag the blue edges to just outside the first and last real entry. Everything shaded red is "not listing".
  3. Tick what is actually in those red strips.
  4. → for the next page.
The edges come pre-positioned, and they are slightly wrong in a predictable way. The guess is the extent of green boxes — lines starting with a ditto mark (" or OCR'd 44), which are nearly always real entries. But a surname block opens with the un-dittoed surname itself, which sits above the first ditto. So the top edge usually needs nudging up a line or two, and entries sometimes run below the bottom guess too. Confirming the guess uncritically is the one way to make these labels worthless.

Surname headers are part of the body — include them

The ALL-CAPS surnames heading each block CALDWELL · CALLAHAN · ACME · ADAMS go inside the blue edges, even though they are not entries themselves.

They are the antecedent for every dittoed entry beneath them: " Harry A reporter h 67 Woodbine only means Callahan Harry A because CALLAHAN sits above it. Cut the header and the whole block below loses its surname — the same failure that keeps alpha_run_filter --apply switched off.

So: top edge above the surname, not above the first " line. If a column's first thing is a surname header, that is where the body starts.

What is in the strips — tick all that apply

1 · ad
Display advertising. Somebody selling something. WILKINSON'S DETECTIVE AGENCY · JOSEPH RYAN, PAINTER AND DECORATOR
This is the one that matters — ad copy is what becomes fabricated people.
2 · furniture
The book's own page furniture: page number, volume title, section head. 202 · Brooklyn Street Directory · UPINGTON'S DIRECTORY OFFICE
Mostly harmless — the text filter already removes most of it.
3 · empty
Nothing there: white space, a rule, OCR noise. Exclusive — ticking it clears the others.

Both an ad and a page number? Tick both. That is the normal case, not an edge case: leaf 13's foot carries the Joseph Ryan ad and the page number 25. The label records what is there; it is not a single verdict.

Head and foot are labelled separately. 123 set the head, shift+ the same keys set the foot. Until you touch them they show grey, meaning "defaulted, nobody looked" — that is recorded in the data.

No listing on the page at all

Press b. The page greys out and you say what the whole leaf is instead:

1 · ad
A full page of display advertising.
2 · front
Front matter — title page, preface, almanac, contents. Before the listing starts.
3 · index
Back matter — the business directory, the street directory, an appendix. After the listing, or between its letter blocks.
4 · blank
Blank leaf, a plate, or too damaged to read.

Irregular — i

Ads woven through the body rather than banded above and below it, so no pair of edges can separate them. Should be rare. Flag it and move on — these are excluded from scoring, and if they turn out common that is itself the finding.

Do not overthink it

Within a line or two is fine. What is being measured is whether the strips get found at all, not whether you agreed with yourself to the pixel. Steady pace beats precision here.

Moving the edges

One edge at a time is active — it is the solid blue one, with a ▸ on its number. ↑↓ move that edge; Tab (or the button in the sidebar) switches to the other. Dragging an edge with the mouse also makes it active, so you can drag roughly and then fine-tune with the arrows.

It starts on the top edge, because that is the one the prefill reliably places too low. w and s always move the top edge whatever is active, if you prefer that to switching.

Saving

You do not save page by page. One save writes every page you have visited, so there is nothing to remember — and it autosaves every 8 seconds whenever something has changed. The sidebar says all saved with a time, or unsaved changes.

Each save rewrites the whole file from what is on screen, so saving often costs nothing. Enter forces one. Stopping part-way is fine: re-run the same command with --resume and your labels come back.
